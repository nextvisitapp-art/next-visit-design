Next Visit family · Design audit · Step 1 of 3

Two products, one brand — and right now they don’t feel like it.

An honest audit of Next Visit (couples) and Go (trip planner) against the current BRAND.md. Findings, evidence, scorecard, and a priority list. No proposals yet — that’s step 2.

The audit in five lines

What I found

  1. Go has quietly become a different brand. The blue primary, the affiliate-colored cards, the serif italic everywhere — it reads like a sibling-shaped-stranger, not the same family. The pink “Save to Next Visit” card at the bottom is the only thing that names the parent.
  2. Couples is mostly on-brand, but emoji is doing all the iconography — for lists, settings, notification rows, quizzes, music. It’s warm, but it’s noisy and brittle, and it puts a hard ceiling on how calm the product can ever feel.
  3. Three off-palette accent surfaces leak through both products. Green (music card, “With Partner” card on settings), purple (Quizzes, Airalo), and the entire affiliate rainbow on Go. BRAND.md is explicit: never use teal, pure black, or grey outside the text variables. Today the rule is broken in at least eight places.
  4. The countdown — your single best emotional moment — is duplicated, not unified. Couples renders it as a pink gradient with floating hearts; Go renders it as a blue gradient with the destination set in Fraunces italic. Same anatomy, different brand. This is the most fixable problem you have.
  5. Cross-product navigation is one button each way, and it works. “Save to Next Visit” on Go and “Plan a new trip” on couples Home. The handoff moment is fine. The visual handshake — i.e. the trip card that lands on couples Home after save — is doing too little to feel rewarding.

MethodologyWhat was looked at, and against what

I reviewed all 53 attached screens and held each one up against the current BRAND.md across eight axes. Where a finding is subjective (tone, density, voice), I’ve said so. Where it’s an explicit rule violation, I’ve called the rule by name.

Axes

Brand consistency · Visual hierarchy · Information density · Mobile UX · Typography · Component patterns · Empty & loading states · Cross-product cohesion.

Scoring

Each axis gets a three-state read: solid   drifting   broken. “Drifting” means it works but isn’t systematized; “broken” means a BRAND.md rule is being violated or the pattern would fail a usability review.

Section 1 — couplesNext Visit: calm, mostly

The voice is on. Voice + tone is the strongest part of the product right now — “the space for the two of you”, “made for you two”, “your story starts here”. The issues are visual, not editorial.

C-01
Onboarding is the cleanest part of the product — and isn’t letting the rest of the app follow its lead
P2

Four screens, single card centered on cream, one decision per screen, copy is calm and specific (“So we can quietly remember it for you.”). Pink primary, pink-tinted card shadow, sentence case. This is what the brand looks like at its best.

Why it matters. The onboarding sets a quality bar that the rest of couples doesn’t quite reach. The Home, Us, and Memories screens should feel like this. Right now they introduce extra colors and emoji that an onboarding flow has the discipline to avoid.

C-02
Three off-brand accent surfaces on Us / Settings
P0

The “Our song” card on Us renders as a green gradient with a Spotify embed inside; the Quizzes card uses a purple/lavender surface; the “With Partner 2” banner on Settings uses a mint/green gradient. Green and purple are not in BRAND.md; mint reads close to teal, which the brand explicitly forbids.

Why it matters. BRAND.md says “never use teal, pure black, or grey outside the text variables.” Today the Us page has three different accent palettes within a single scroll. This is the single biggest visual reason couples doesn’t feel as quiet as the brand voice claims.

C-03
Emoji is doing all the iconography
P1

Every list, every settings row, every quiz tile, every category card uses an emoji as its visual anchor — heart, party-popper, sun, sparkle, lightbulb, dice, baby, money-bag, swirl, telephone, clapper, lock. Eleven lists. Six notification rows. Four quiz tiles. They’re inconsistent (some flat, some 3D, some color, some not) and they render differently across operating systems.

Why it matters. Emoji is fine as a tonal grace note (the brand mark is 💕). It’s the wrong primary iconography for a product whose ambition is “calm, intentional, thoughtful friend.” A unified monoline icon set, with pink as the only accent, would buy the product more calm than any other single change.

C-04
Hero treatments on Home are doing three different things
P1

Before a trip: a pink-to-pink-soft gradient card with two floating 3D hearts and a white “Plan a trip ✨” button (button is white, pink text, sparkle emoji). After a trip: a more saturated pink card with “19 days to go”, no hearts, a pill-shaped “Trip details” dropdown. Directly below the countdown sits a third pink-gradient card — “Plan a new trip” — that looks visually identical to the hero. Three pink-on-pink cards stacked.

Why it matters. A hero should be one thing. Right now “hero” means three different cards on three different states, and once a trip is saved they all compete. Only the countdown deserves hero status; the others should drop to secondary-card density.

C-05
Input styling is inconsistent
P2

The name input on onboarding has a pink hairline border. The birthday picker has no border and a grey fill. The partner-code input has a thicker pink border than the name input. The Where input on Add Memory has a pink border; the From/To dates next to it are borderless. Within a single form the input style varies.

Why it matters. BRAND.md gives 10px radius for inputs and a default border token. One input token, applied everywhere, fixes this in a day.

C-06
Memories index introduces a black/dark segmented control
P1

The Timeline / Gallery / Map switcher on the Memories index uses a solid black pill for the active state. BRAND.md: “Never use pure black.” The same dark-pill pattern appears on the trip detail tabs on Go (Flights / Stay / Things to do / Prep).

Why it matters. This is the only place a near-black appears in the active state. A pink-tinted ink (or a soft pink fill with pink text) would fix it cleanly and match the rest of the system.

C-07
Photos / story is in transition and currently shows only an empty state
P1

“Your story starts here” — a single card, single CTA, one camera glyph. Lovely empty state, on-brand voice. But the “hybrid capture model (unsorted + tagged sections)” that you flagged in the brief is not visible anywhere in these screens. The Add bottom-sheet shows two options (“Add photos” / “Add memory”) with two more off-palette icon tiles (blue, green).

Why it matters. The empty state is fine; what’s missing is the populated state, the Unsorted bucket, and the tagging affordance. We’ll need to design these from scratch in step 3 — there’s nothing to audit yet.

C-08
Footer chrome and wordmark drift
P2

Top-right on Home: “Next Visit 💕” — sans serif, bold, sentence case, brand-mark glued to the end. On Go: “Next Visit” — italic accent on the second word, no heart. Two different wordmarks for one product family. Couples also adds tiny grey footer text (“Made for the two of you”, “Next Visit · made for you two”) which is sweet but duplicated on every screen.

Why it matters. The wordmark is the cheapest unification you can do. One mark, used identically in both products, with one consistent position.

Section 2 — goGo: a different brand, wearing the same wordmark

Go is the bigger problem. It’s a well-built planner; the flow is good, the AI windows are genuinely clever, the affiliate density is appropriate to the job. But it doesn’t look like Next Visit anymore. It looks like a confident neighbor.

G-01
The blue primary rebrand has eaten the brand
P0

Primary CTAs (“Next — Where?”, “Build my trip”), step indicators (1/2/3 circles in pale blue), progress bars, focused-card outlines (the “Solo” selection card on step 1), the “See accommodations” button, the entire “You’re all set” trip-detail hero, the flight cards, the stop counter chips — all blue. The serif italic accent words in titles also render in blue. The pink primary appears only in the bottom “Save to Next Visit” card on the last screen.

Why it matters. BRAND.md: Primary #F43F6A. Today Go ships with ~#0770E3 as its primary. A user who clicks “Save to Next Visit” goes from a blue product to a pink product and has to do the brand reconciliation in their head. Killing this is the single highest-leverage change in the audit.

G-02
Affiliate cards are a brand-color rainbow
P0

Stay section: navy Booking.com card. Things-to-do: red Klook card, green Viator card, teal GetYourGuide card. Before-you-go: orange DiscoverCars, purple Airalo, dark green SafetyWing. Seven full-bleed colored cards in a single scroll.

Why it matters. Three rules broken in one block: teal is named-banned in BRAND.md, the page is not calm, and the affiliate brand outranks Next Visit’s own brand inside its own product. There’s an obvious move here — neutral cards with a small monogram or wordmark in the corner — but I’ll save that for step 2.

G-03
Gradient dashed-border “AI” buttons are a third button pattern
P1

“Find us somewhere”, “Find cheapest windows”, “Find my neighbourhood”, “Pick top activities for us”, “Find us a different route”, “Try again”: all rendered as a pink-to-yellow gradient pill with an orange dashed border and a sparkle emoji. They’re lovely, but they’re a third button style alongside primary (blue today, pink in the future) and secondary (white). Six of them appear in the planning flow.

Why it matters. A distinct “AI suggestion” pattern is a good idea. The execution borrows yellow (also off-palette) and the orange dashed border reads like a system warning. Keep the idea, redo the surface in pink + cream with a soft sparkle.

G-04
Headlines use Fraunces italic inline accent — and it works
P2

“Skip the planning.” · “Where to next?” · “When are you going?” · “Three ideas for you”. The italic accent word at the end of each title is a really nice device. It also shows up on couples (“Our little corner”, “Add a memory”, “Our anniversary”).

Why it matters. This is a real shared device across both products. Two things to fix: the accent color today is blue on Go and pink on couples; make it pink everywhere. And codify it (1–3 words, always at the end, never on a body sentence).

G-05
The countdown is the right idea, in the wrong color
P0

“You’re all set” → big blue gradient card with destination set in Fraunces italic + sans, date range, NN days to go, two outlined buttons (Copy as text / Share link), tabs below. Same anatomy as couples Home countdown, just blue.

Why it matters. This is the most emotionally important moment in either product, and it’s the most fixable. One countdown component, one color, one typography lockup, used in both products. If we do nothing else, do this.

G-06
Results page works hard; some of the work isn’t legible
P1

“Three ideas for you” — full-bleed image, title row with city/country, italic one-line description, three KPI columns (Flight / Est. cost / Best time), and 4 highlight chips. Density is right for the job; readability of the KPI row is borderline at mobile width (three columns × small caps label × small black value). Chips below are light blue pills (off-palette) with subtle borders.

Why it matters. This is the “efficient planner” tone working correctly — but the chips need to be a single brand color (or neutral) and the KPI row needs more vertical air. Easy fixes.

G-07
Save-to-Next-Visit card is the one piece of brand glue on Go
P2

Bottom of trip detail: pink gradient card, “DON’T LOSE THIS TRIP”, “Plan it together →”, white “Save to Next Visit” button with pink text and the 💕 mark, three-bullet footer (Free · Just an email · Built for couples). Voice is correct. Visual is correct. It works.

Why it matters. This card and the “JUST SAVED” banner on couples Home (s27) are the only two visual handshakes between products. Both currently work in isolation; together they could be choreographed to feel like a single moment.

G-08
Step indicators, progress bars, and number badges use four different blue tones
P1

The thick filled progress bar segment is a saturated cobalt. The pale outline progress segments are a light blue. The “1, 2, 3” circular section badges are a desaturated lavender-blue. The card outlines on selection are yet another blue. None of them is the same blue as the primary button.

Why it matters. Worth flagging because it’s how the blue rebrand snuck in: not one color, but a small system of them. When we move to pink, one tonal scale (pink-100 → pink-700) covers all four cases.

Section 3 — cohesionWhere the two products meet

There are three places the products touch: the wordmark, the handoff card on Go, and the trip card on couples Home after save. The first is the weakest; the second is good; the third is missing the moment.

X-01
Two wordmarks, one brand
P1

Couples Home: Next Visit + 💕, top-right, sans serif. Go: Next Visit, top-left, italic-accent treatment, no mark. Different position, different typography, different presence of the brand glyph.

X-02
The handoff moment from go → couples is half-built
P1

On Go, “Save to Next Visit” lives in a beautifully on-brand pink card at the end of the trip. On couples Home after save, the result is a slim banner “JUST SAVED · Chiang Mai, Thailand · View trip · ×” sitting above the now-populated countdown. The countdown itself is the reward; the banner is doing nothing for the moment.

Why it matters. “Trip arrives on couples Home” is the emotional payoff of the whole cross-product loop. It should be a designed moment, not a banner.

X-03
The reverse handoff (couples → go) is currently a domain disclosure
P2

The “Plan a new trip” card on Home reads: “Flights, stays, things to do — on go.next-visit.app”. And the small tile below repeats: “Plan a trip · Opens go.next-visit.app”. The voice exposes the subdomain to the user, which is a developer instinct, not a brand instinct.

Why it matters. Per BRAND.md voice (“like a thoughtful friend”), “opens our planner” or simply nothing is correct. The subdomain is plumbing.

Section 5 — priorityWhat ships first

Ranked by impact-per-week-of-work. P0 = brand-blocking, must fix before any new feature. P1 = inconsistency that’s costing you a lot of calm. P2 = polish, do in batches.

P0 · brand-blocking

Kill the blue primary on Go G-01

Replace #0770E3 with #F43F6A across primary buttons, step indicators, progress bars, selection outlines, the “You’re all set” hero, and the flight cards. Single largest visual change; restores brand instantly.

Neutralize the affiliate-rainbow cards G-02

Move all seven partner cards to a single neutral surface (white or cream with a hairline pink border). Keep the partner mark as a small monogram or wordmark in the corner; drop the gradient fills.

Unify the countdown G-05

One countdown component, pink gradient, used identically on couples Home and the Go trip-detail page. This is the most emotionally important screen in either product.

Retire the off-palette accent cards on Us / Settings C-02

Green music card, purple Quizzes card, mint “with partner” banner. All three move to white-on-cream with a single pink accent rule.

P1 · inconsistency tax

Replace emoji iconography with a single monoline icon set C-03

Lists, settings rows, notification rows, quiz tiles, category cards. Keep 💕 as the brand glyph; everywhere else gets a single icon family.

Resolve Home hero into a single component with three states C-04

Pre-trip → empty (gentle), upcoming-trip → countdown, in-trip → live (later). Stop stacking three pink-on-pink cards.

Redesign the “AI suggestion” button pattern G-03

Keep the idea of a distinct AI/serendipity CTA. Lose the yellow gradient and the orange dashed border; they read warning-amber, not magical.

Replace the black segmented active state C-06

One segmented-control component, pink-tinted active state, used on Memories and on Go trip-detail tabs.

Choreograph the save-to-couples landing moment X-02

Replace the slim “JUST SAVED” banner with a real arrival moment on couples Home. This is the cross-product payoff.

One wordmark, one position X-01, C-08

Pick a single Next Visit wordmark (with or without 💕 — recommend with) and use it identically in both products.

Collapse the four Go blues into one pink scale G-08

Progress bar / pill / step badge / selection outline all map to the same pink-100 → pink-700 ramp.

P2 · polish

One input style across both products C-05

10px radius, pink hairline border, focused state inherits the brand accent.

Codify the italic-accent headline rule G-04

1–3 word italic Fraunces accent at the end of a sentence-case headline, always pink, never on body text.

Loosen the results-card KPI row G-06

More vertical air, single chip color, larger value type at mobile width.

Remove the subdomain disclosure on couples Home X-03

“Opens our planner” or nothing. The subdomain is plumbing, not voice.
