Next Visit family · Unified system proposal · Step 2 of 3

One brand, two voices, shared primitives.

A unified design system that locks the brand without flattening the products. Calm for couples, efficient for go — same palette, same type, same scale, different density and chrome. Apple Photos vs Apple Wallet, not two different apps.

Approved directionTwo visual variants of one set of primitives
Icon familyPhosphor (regular + duotone)
Tech constraintsCouples React/Vite · Go single-file vanilla · Mobile-first

Section 1 — principleWhat’s shared, what’s split

The unification works because we’re strict about what counts as “the brand” vs “the product expression.” Five things lock; five things flex. If a thing isn’t on the left list, it can vary by product. If it’s on the left, it cannot.

Shared — never varies

  • 01
    Color palette. The pink ramp, cream/white surfaces, ink scale. No brand-specific accents. No blue.
  • 02
    Typography. Fraunces headlines, system sans body, JetBrains Mono labels. Same scale ratios.
  • 03
    Primitives. Button, card, input, chip, segmented. Identical anatomy, identical states.
  • 04
    Iconography. Phosphor, regular weight. Same library, same stroke.
  • 05
    The countdown. One component, one color, one anatomy. Shipped in both.

Variant — can flex per product

  • 01
    Density. Couples uses the loose padding scale (24/32/48). Go uses the tight scale (12/16/24).
  • 02
    Hero treatment. Couples = floating hearts + soft type. Go = destination photography above the same gradient card.
  • 03
    Imagery. Couples = sparse, partner-uploaded photos as memory. Go = stock destination photography in results & trip detail.
  • 04
    Information per screen. Couples = one decision. Go = a flow with optional shortcuts.
  • 05
    Voice register. Couples = thoughtful friend. Go = knowledgeable friend. Same person, different mood.

Voice split, in lockstep

Same writer, different room. Both products avoid the BRAND.md banned phrases. The split is in cadence, not vocabulary.

Couples (calm)

Cadence

Short sentences. Specific over generic. Names the moment.

Examples

“Your story starts here.”

“So we can quietly remember it for you.”

“The space for the two of you.”

Go (efficient)

Cadence

Verbs first. Number of seconds, number of options. Useful, not warm.

Examples

“Skip the planning. A trip in 30 seconds.”

“Three ideas, from BNE, ~$250/day.”

“Cheapest window: 2 June → 9 June.”

Italic accent rule

The italic-Fraunces accent word at the end of a sentence-case headline is the brand’s strongest signature device. Codified: 1–3 words, always at the end of the line, always pink, never on body text or button labels, never repeated within a single screen.

Section 4 — iconographyPhosphor, regular weight

Per your call: warm not corporate. Phosphor at regular weight is the right pick — friendlier than Lucide, more humble than Heroicons. Stroke is round-ended, terminals are gentle, the geometry is soft. 💕 stays as the brand glyph; the rest of the product uses Phosphor.
